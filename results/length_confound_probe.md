# Is the NLI clustering length-driven? (2026-08-13)

Generated by `scripts/length_confound_probe{,2,3,4}.py` (CPU only; no GPU, no model, no
re-clustering). Reproduces and then decomposes the out-of-scope measurement raised while
costing the judge's owed validation conditions.

## 0. Verdict

**Real, and not previously disclosed — but the direction is the opposite of the worry.**

The split rate *is* strongly length-driven (0.157 at short pairs, 0.948 at long ones).
Length *is* correlated with the correctness label, so it is a genuine confound and not a
common-mode effect that cancels. But the clean separation **survives** length control, and
a model-free clusterer on *truncated* text separates the strata **significantly better**
than the deployed NLI clusterer does on full text. The 48-token verbose setting is
**depressing** the reported detection power, not manufacturing it.

Three things follow, in descending order of how much they cost:

1. **The paper has no length baseline, and needs one.** Mean generation length alone scores
   AUROC 0.634 on the fair pool against semantic entropy's 0.704. SE beats it — paired
   delta +0.070 [+0.024, +0.119] — but a reader is entitled to see that number, and it is
   nowhere in the repo.
2. **Two undisclosed deviations from the source method.** The repo clusters raw generations;
   Kuhn/Farquhar concatenate the question with each answer before the NLI call, and prompt
   for short answers. Neither deviation is stated in the paper.
3. **One genuinely open risk, on the attack claims only.** Whether the false-alarm attack
   wins partly by *lengthening* generations cannot be answered from cache — the attack cells
   store scores, not the attacked generations. ~20 GPU-min to settle.

Nothing here forces a retraction. Item 3 is the only one that could.

## 1. Reproduction

Independently reproduced from the cache, not read off `judge_owed_conditions.py`:

| population | n | oracle-positive pairs | NLI splits them |
|---|---|---|---|
| whole pool | 2000 | 51,914 | 32,957 = **0.635** [0.631, 0.639] |
| FA-80 targets (`wk9_defb`) | 80 | 2,748 | 1,826 = **0.664** [0.647, 0.682] |

Matches the reported 63.5% / 66.4% exactly.

## 2. The split rate is length-driven — confirmed

Oracle-positive pairs only (both samples contain a gold alias, span oracle):

| max(words) of the pair | n | split rate |
|---|---|---|
| 0–9 | 5,787 | 0.157 [0.148, 0.167] |
| 10–19 | 20,300 | 0.447 [0.440, 0.453] |
| 20–29 | 9,128 | 0.818 [0.810, 0.826] |
| 30–39 | 14,313 | 0.926 [0.922, 0.930] |
| 40+ | 2,386 | 0.948 [0.938, 0.956] |

A 6× swing. Cap-truncation compounds it — 25.4% of cached samples end without terminal
punctuation (they were cut at `max_new_tokens=48`):

| | n | split rate |
|---|---|---|
| neither truncated | 35,415 | 0.522 [0.517, 0.527] |
| one truncated | 12,557 | 0.874 [0.868, 0.880] |
| both truncated | 3,942 | 0.888 [0.878, 0.897] |

**The clusterer is not simply broken**: pairs whose text is identical after normalisation
split at 0.002 [0.001, 0.003] (n=6,540).

**Oracle coarseness is not the driver.** "Oracle-positive" only means both samples *contain*
a gold alias, so for gold `Cancer` the samples "died of breast cancer" and "died of ovarian
cancer" are both positive and are genuinely different answers — a split there is the
clusterer being right. If that coarseness drove the rate, longer and more specific golds
would split *less*. They split *more* (1 word 0.611 → 3+ words 0.696), so the gradient runs
the wrong way for that explanation.

## 3. Q1 — expected under Farquhar et al.? **No. Two deviations.**

**(a) No question-conditioning.** `se.entropy.cluster_samples` (src/se/entropy.py:41) builds
pairs straight from the raw samples:

```python
pairs = [(samples[i], samples[j]) for i, j in combinations(range(n), 2)]
```

The source method conditions the entailment check on the question: *"the question is
concatenated to each generation and then both concatenations are concatenated together using
a special token"* — Kuhn et al.'s bidirectional-entailment algorithm, carried into Farquhar
et al. This repo compares bare answer strings. `methods.tex:5-9` describes the clustering as
"$y_i$ and $y_j$ share a cluster iff each entails the other under the NLI model", which
matches the code and not the source.

**(b) No brevity constraint or answer extraction.** The SE short-form QA setting prompts the
model to answer "as briefly as possible", yielding answers averaging ~15 *characters*. This
repo generates zero-shot from the Llama chat template at `max_new_tokens=48` with no brevity
instruction and no extraction step, yielding **19.6 words / ~110 characters** — roughly an
order of magnitude longer, with a quarter of them cut mid-sentence.

So the long-generation regime that produces the 63.5% is a property of this repo's
configuration, not of the method. `config.py:34-38` already pins 48 tokens *for consistency
across conditions* and cites "external review §7" — but that guards against a length
*inconsistency between arms*, which is a different concern from length being confounded with
the label.

## 4. Q2 — does it touch a live claim?

### The load-bearing test

Every live claim is a **difference** between the correct and hallucinating strata. A length
effect common to both cancels in that difference. So the question is not the split rate at
all — it is whether length predicts the label.

It does:

| | correct (n=1424) | hallucinating (n=576) |
|---|---|---|
| mean words/sample | 18.76 | 21.63 (**+2.87**) |

| detector | full pool | fair pool |
|---|---|---|
| semantic entropy | 0.6937 [0.6695, 0.7172] | **0.7043** [0.6567, 0.7546] |
| mean generation length alone | 0.6311 [0.6065, 0.6567] | 0.6344 |
| paired delta (SE − length) | +0.0627 [+0.0407, +0.0860] | +0.0698 [+0.0236, +0.1193] |

Spearman corr(mean length, K) = **+0.666**. So the clustering is substantially a length
measurement — but SE still beats the length baseline, significantly, on both pools.

### Does the separation survive length control? Yes

Length-stratified AUROC — correct vs hallucinating compared **only within a length bin**,
pooled over concordant pairs, so a length effect common to both strata cannot contribute:

| bins | stratified AUROC (full pool) |
|---|---|
| 5 | 0.6439 |
| 10 | 0.6375 |
| 20 | 0.6373 |

Fair pool, 5 bins: 0.6559. Against 0.5 for a detector with no signal. The separation is
real and is not an artefact of length; it is *attenuated* by controlling for length
(0.694 → 0.637), which is expected when the control is itself partly signal.

## 5. The finding that inverts the worry

Truncating the cached text to its first sentence — then clustering by the repo's own
model-free exact-match relation (`se.entropy.cluster_samples_exact`, verified with
`se.scoring.normalize_answer`) — **beats the deployed NLI clusterer**:

| detector | full pool | fair pool |
|---|---|---|
| NLI clusterer, full text (deployed) | 0.6937 | 0.7043 |
| exact-match, full text | 0.7240 (+0.030 [+0.009, +0.051]) | 0.7579 (+0.054 [+0.014, +0.095]) |
| **exact-match, first sentence** | **0.7625** (+0.069 [+0.046, +0.091]) | **0.7918** (+0.088 [+0.046, +0.132]) |
| exact-match, first 10 words | 0.7559 (+0.062 [+0.035, +0.088]) | 0.7862 (+0.081 [+0.031, +0.133]) |

All deltas are paired bootstraps over jointly-resampled questions; all are significant.
Length-stratified, the same ordering holds and widens (0.663 full text → 0.751 at 10 words).

This rules out the reading that verbosity inflates the reported separation. It **depresses**
it. It also bears on the paper's own unexplained gap: `limitations.tex:117-123` reports SE at
0.694 against the literature's 0.828 and declines to attribute the 0.134. A meaningful share
of that gap is plausibly the generation regime — restore short answers and a *model-free*
clusterer reaches 0.76–0.79.

Two honest caveats. Exact-match is a deliberately stricter lower-bound clusterer; its
advantage here does not establish that a question-conditioned NLI on truncated text would do
better still, only that the current configuration is leaving power on the table. And this is
a *clean-detection* result — it does not contradict `methods.tex:293` ("exact-match
saturates"), which is about exact-match as an *adjudicator of attack-induced change*, a
different measurement.

Note the length gap survives first-sentence truncation (+2.89 words) and only collapses under
a hard word cap (+0.29 at 10 words, +0.02 at 5). First-sentence truncation removes the
rambling but not the verbosity difference.

## 6. The one open risk: the attack cells

`wk9_defb/triviaqa_se_false_alarm.jsonl` stores `entropy_before`/`entropy_after`,
`best_query`, `answer_under_q_prime` — **but not the 10 attacked generations**. So whether
the false-alarm entropy move is partly a *length* move cannot be answered from cache.

What the cache does license, on the 80 FA targets:
- corr(benign length, benign H) = +0.544
- corr(benign length, attack entropy move) = **−0.252** (n=80)

The negative sign is weakly consistent with "the attack wins where generations were short,
i.e. had room to lengthen" — suggestive, not decisive, and also consistent with simple
regression to the mean.

This is the highest-value measurement the length question implies, and it is cheap:
re-generating 10 samples under each winning `q'` is 80 targets × ~6.1 s ≈ **8 GPU-min** per
cell, under 20 min for both. **Costed, not launched.**

## 7. Q3 — already disclosed? **No.**

Searched `docs/critique_log.md`, `docs/decisions.md`, `results/*.md`, `paper/sections/*.tex`:

- `config.py:34-38` + `docs/critic_charter.md:40` — "pinned generation length across all
  conditions". This is length *consistency between arms*, not length confounded with the label.
- `results/wk3_wed_nli_check.md:26` — hand-crafted NLI accuracy 11/16, and the failures are
  exactly this mode (row 14, "additional consistent detail": expected equivalent, NLI says no,
  0.00 one way / 1.00 the other). **The mechanism was measured in Week 3 and never connected
  to the clean AUROC.**
- `judge_owed_conditions.py:207-211` — the docstring already warns that oracle-positive is not
  bidirectional entailment. Correct, and it bounds §1, but it does not raise the label confound.
- `docs/critique_log.md:75-76` — span-match was chosen *over* extraction+EM, deliberately. That
  decision is about the **oracle**, not the **clusterer**; it does not cover this.
- No length baseline, no length-vs-label measurement, and no statement of either deviation in
  §3 exists anywhere in the repo.

`experiments.tex:9` describes generation as "N=10 answers at temperature 1.0" — the 48-token
cap and the absence of brevity prompting are not stated in the paper at all.
