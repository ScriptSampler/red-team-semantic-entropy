\paragraph{Selection on a noisy estimator.}
Semantic entropy is a finite-sample quantity: for each candidate we cluster
$N{=}10$ sampled answers, so the score is a Monte-Carlo estimate of the true
entropy. Our optimiser selects the extremum of this estimate over $\sim$180
candidate paraphrases, and the extremum of many noisy-but-unbiased estimates is
biased in the attacker's favour even when, as here, sampling is seeded so each
estimate is \emph{reproducible}. Reproducibility is not the same as noise-freeness.
We therefore do not report raw attack effect sizes as the headline; we report attack
success and AUROC degradation \emph{net of a noise floor} obtained by scoring $K$
random, non-adversarial NLI-passing paraphrases of each target and taking the best
move they achieve by chance. An effect that does not exceed this floor is not
claimed. This control distinguishes genuine manipulation from selection-on-noise; we are
not aware of it in prior paraphrase-attack evaluations of these detectors (a claim
we will verify against the literature at revision).

\paragraph{The clusterer and the equivalence gate share one NLI model.}
Semantic entropy is \emph{defined} by DeBERTa-large-MNLI bidirectional clustering,
and our attack's feasibility constraint uses the same model, so a measured degradation
could in part reflect that one model's self-inconsistency rather than a paradigm-level
weakness. We address this directly by re-clustering the answer samples under equivalence
oracles independent of the DeBERTa-MNLI supervision (Methods), but two caveats bound the
check. First, the cheap independent oracles are unreliable on the adversarial case
(exact-match saturates; the sentence embedder is near-chance), so the adjudication rests
on a single LLM-judge; while that judge is validated ($0.92$) on the hard discrimination
the others fail, one oracle---even independent and validated---is not dispositive, and a
human or multi-judge equivalence audit remains owed. Second, the judge's own
positive-recognition is imperfect (it slightly over-splits genuine aliases), which makes
its adjudication \emph{conservative} for the false-alarm direction but not necessarily for
hide; and our validation labels (TriviaQA alias lists) carry noise that the judge, in
places, more than corrects for. The independent-clusterer result is therefore reported as preliminary
evidence, pending the confirmatory $n{\geq}80$ run.

\paragraph{Equivalence is certified automatically, not by humans.}
Feasibility is a bidirectional-entailment judgement, and our answer-flip
subcategory---successful attacks in which the model's correctness flips under
$q'$---is a \emph{lower} bound on how often the gate admits a non-equivalent
paraphrase, since a $q'$ can shift scope or presupposition while leaving the greedy
answer unchanged. We do not yet report a human (or disclosed strong-LLM-judge)
equivalence audit of a random sample of successful $q'$; such an audit, with an
inter-annotator agreement estimate, is needed before the meaning-preserving claim is
fully established.

\paragraph{Status check versus what the detector sees.}
The success criterion re-checks the model's answer with a single greedy decode, while
the detector clusters temperature-$1.0$ samples. The two can disagree---a greedy
answer may stay wrong while the sampled distribution shifts toward correct (or vice
versa)---so we report both the greedy status and the fraction of the detector's own
samples that are correct under $q'$, rather than relying on the greedy check alone.

\paragraph{Model, precision, data, and budget.}
Results are on a single model family (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) run in 4-bit
quantisation on one 16\,GB GPU; quantisation may shift both the entropy estimates and
the attack surface relative to a full-precision detector, and generalisation across
model families and scales is untested. Our primary dataset is TriviaQA; broader
datasets (e.g.\ SQuAD) are in progress. The attack budget (beam and iteration counts)
is modest by necessity, and the reported cells use small per-stratum $n$, so the
confidence intervals are wide and the numbers are best read as preliminary effect
sizes rather than tight estimates.

\paragraph{SRE fidelity and threat model.}
We reproduce SRE with exact-match-then-NLI-union-find clustering rather than the
paper's full energy-based hybrid, and our NLI backend (deberta-large-mnli) is
weaker than the deberta-v2-xlarge used in some prior work: our \emph{SE} replication
reaches AUROC $0.787$ against the paper's SE figure of $0.828$, and the SRE target
(paper $0.871$) depends further on the clustering backend we simplified. Because SRE
re-paraphrases its input
internally, the attacker's chosen $q'$ is only one input to a detector that then
generates its own reformulations; the SRE threat model (whether the attacker controls
the reformulation set) needs to be stated and matched to the implementation, which we
flag as a limitation of the SRE results specifically.
