\paragraph{Why the attack works.}
Semantic entropy measures uncertainty over \emph{meaning} by clustering sampled
answers with an NLI equivalence relation. Its foundational assumption is that this
meaning is a property of the question, invariant to how the question is phrased. Our
attack targets exactly that assumption: a paraphrase $q'$ that an NLI model still
calls equivalent to $q$ can nonetheless shift the target model's answer-sampling
distribution, and hence the induced cluster structure and its entropy. The
false-alarm direction exploits the detector's tendency to over-fragment a correct
answer that can be expressed many ways; the hide direction exploits the mirror,
collapsing a wrong answer's diverse phrasings toward apparent consensus. In both
cases we hypothesise the manipulation lives in the gap between two notions of
equivalence---the NLI gate's and the sampling distribution's---that the detector
implicitly assumes coincide; isolating that this gap is the cause (rather than a
correlate) is exactly what the independent-clusterer check would establish.

\paragraph{Is the weakness structural?}
We conjecture the mechanism generalises beyond one implementation: any detector that
operationalises ``semantic equivalence'' through a learned model inherits that
model's decision boundary as an attack surface---the very component that defines the
score is the component an attacker steers. This \emph{a-priori} argument follows from
the construction alone. Establishing it \emph{empirically} is harder than it first
looks. Transfer between SE and a reformulation-based variant (SRE) is suggestive but
confounded: in our setup both detectors share the same DeBERTa-large-MNLI backbone,
so a paraphrase that moves both may be exploiting one model's shared blind spot
rather than a paradigm-level property. The load-bearing test is therefore an
\emph{independent} clustering oracle (Limitations): if the degradation survives a
different equivalence model, the weakness is structural; if it does not, it is that
NLI model's self-inconsistency. We report SE-to-SRE transfer as evidence
\emph{consistent with} the structural hypothesis, not as proof of it.

\paragraph{Why lead with false alarms.}
Prior red-teaming of uncertainty estimators emphasises the hide direction (missed
hallucinations). We foreground false alarms because, for a deployed abstention or
flagging system, systematically inflating the uncertainty of \emph{correct} answers
is its own failure mode: it erodes utility and user trust, and it is cheaper for an
adversary who merely wants to make a model look unreliable. That this direction is
attackable at all is, we argue, the more surprising and under-examined result.

\paragraph{Defence.}
Averaging the detector score over several reformulations of whatever input is
received---the mechanism SRE already builds in---should dilute a single adversarial
phrasing. Our defence experiment asks whether this confers measurable robustness or
merely raises the attacker's query cost; either way, a detector whose own
reformulation step is its best defence suggests that robustness to paraphrase must be
designed in, not assumed. We frame all of the above against a null noise floor, so
the claims are about effects that exceed what benign rephrasing achieves by chance.
