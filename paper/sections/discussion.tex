\paragraph{Hypothesised mechanism.}
Semantic entropy measures uncertainty over \emph{meaning} by clustering sampled
answers with an NLI equivalence relation. Its foundational assumption is that this
meaning is a property of the question, invariant to how the question is phrased. Our
attack targets exactly that assumption: a paraphrase $q'$ that an NLI model still
calls equivalent to $q$ can nonetheless shift the target model's answer-sampling
distribution, and hence the induced cluster structure and its entropy. The
false-alarm direction exploits the detector's tendency to over-fragment a correct
answer that can be expressed many ways; the hide direction exploits the mirror,
collapsing a wrong answer's diverse phrasings toward apparent consensus. In both
cases we hypothesise the manipulation lives in the gap between two notions of
equivalence---the NLI gate's and the sampling distribution's---that the detector
implicitly assumes coincide; isolating that this gap is the cause (rather than a
correlate) is exactly what the independent-clusterer check would establish.

\paragraph{How much of this is an attack, and how much is the detector?}
The mechanism above presumes there is room to move the score. At the top of the scale there
often is not, and that changes what the false-alarm result is a result \emph{about}. We
should be precise about the scope: on our score-independent pool the detector separates
correct from hallucinating answers moderately well (AUROC $0.704$ [$0.653$, $0.753$]), so
this is not a claim that semantic entropy fails as a detector. It is a claim about one
region of its range. A tenth of correct answers are already at the estimator's maximum
before anything is done to them, a quarter are within the top decile, and the estimator
offers only $22$ distinct values in which to express any difference---and it is exactly into
that compressed region that a false alarm must be pushed.

The sharpest way to put the finding is therefore not ``we constructed an attack that defeats
semantic entropy'' but ``semantic entropy has so little usable range at the top of its scale
that the false-alarm direction is largely unmeasurable in the detector's own units.'' The
first claim invites the reply that a stronger detector, or a stronger optimiser, would settle
the matter; the second does not, because the natural rebuttal---use an estimator with more
range---concedes the point. Whether the remaining, uncensored margin is one an adversary can
reliably exploit is the question the confirmatory evaluation answers, and we report that
result with the same prominence whichever way it falls.

That reading also disciplines what we may say about magnitude. Because roughly half of
attacked targets end at the cap, the attack and any other method that also saturates become
indistinguishable there, and effect sizes expressed in nats are censored. And because the
reported effect is the maximum over a large candidate search, a further part of it is
selection on estimator noise---measurably about half, on our own data. Strip both away and
what remains is a modest, real, but heavily conditioned quantity. We think the honest
summary is that the vulnerability is real and the \emph{margin} is small, and that most of
the apparent margin in an uncorrected report is an artefact of how the number was obtained.

\paragraph{Is the weakness structural?}
We conjecture the mechanism generalises beyond one implementation: any detector that
operationalises ``semantic equivalence'' through a learned model inherits that
model's decision boundary as an attack surface---the very component that defines the
score is the component an attacker steers. This \emph{a-priori} argument follows from
the construction alone; the load-bearing \emph{empirical} test is whether the effect
survives an equivalence oracle independent of the detector's own NLI. We run that test.
Cheap independent oracles do not settle it---exact-match saturates on short factoid
answers and sentence embedders are near-chance on the adversarial, word-preserving case
(Methods)---but a victim- and NLI-independent LLM-judge, validated to $0.93$ accuracy on
the gold-alias hard negatives that proxy that adversarial discrimination (where the
embedder scores $0.51$), can---for the false-alarm direction, under the disclosed
re-scoped gate (Methods). Under it,
the point estimates lean toward the structural reading: the attack's move ($+0.37$ nats)
sits close to its move under the detector's own NLI ($+0.46$) and far above the saturated
exact-match ($+0.05$), placing it at nearly the same rank within the benign-move
distribution as under the detector's own clusterer. But at the current sample size the
interval is uninformative, so this is the \emph{first evidence bearing on} the structural
question, not a resolution of it; the confirmatory $n{\geq}80$ run now under way is the
gate on the claim. A single validated judge, however independent, is in any case not the
last word; SE-to-SRE transfer, confounded by the shared DeBERTa backbone, is at best
corroborating.

\paragraph{Why lead with false alarms.}
Prior red-teaming of uncertainty estimators emphasises the hide direction (missed
hallucinations). We foreground false alarms because, for a deployed abstention or
flagging system, systematically inflating the uncertainty of \emph{correct} answers
is its own failure mode: it erodes utility and user trust, and it is cheaper for an
adversary who merely wants to make a model look unreliable. That this direction is
attackable at all---should the preliminary effect survive the confirmatory run---would
be, we argue, the more surprising and under-examined result.

\paragraph{Defence.}
Averaging the detector score over several reformulations of whatever input is
received---the mechanism SRE already builds in---should dilute a single adversarial
phrasing. Our defence experiment asks whether this confers measurable robustness or
merely raises the attacker's query cost; either way, a detector whose own
reformulation step is its best defence suggests that robustness to paraphrase must be
designed in, not assumed. We frame all of the above against a null noise floor, so
the claims are about effects that exceed what benign rephrasing achieves by chance.
