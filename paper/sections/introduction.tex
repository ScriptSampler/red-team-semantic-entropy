Large language models are increasingly paired with \emph{hallucination detectors}
that estimate whether a generated answer can be trusted. A prominent family is
sampling-based: semantic entropy (SE)~\citep{kuhn2023semantic, farquhar2024detecting}
samples several answers, clusters them by meaning with a natural-language-inference
(NLI) equivalence check, and reports the entropy of the resulting cluster
distribution; semantic reformulation entropy (SRE)~\citep{tong2025sre} extends this
with input reformulations. These detectors are deployed on an implicit promise: that
the uncertainty they report is a property of the \emph{question and answer}, invariant
to how the question happens to be phrased.

That invariance has been asserted but not adversarially tested \emph{on the detector
itself}. Prior red-teaming of LLMs targets the model's answer---eliciting
hallucinations~\citep{liang2025seca, liang2026realista}---or evades text-origin
classifiers by paraphrasing~\citep{cheng2025advparaphrasing, fang2025copa}; work on
uncertainty manipulation has used fine-tuning or backdoors rather than a
meaning-preserving input change~\citep{uncertaintyfragile2024}, and the closest
detector-side attack degrades a hidden-state probe model-side~\citep{corvus2026}.
Uncertainty \emph{is} attackable from the input side: \citet{obadinma2025verbal} show that
semantics-preserving perturbations degrade an LLM's \emph{verbal} confidence. We therefore
scope the gap narrowly. What remains untested is the \emph{sampling-based} family, whose
score is not a self-report but a statistic of an answer distribution partitioned by a
learned equivalence relation---so the attack surface is that sampling-and-clustering
pipeline rather than a model's self-assessment. The
question we ask is accordingly: can an attacker who only \emph{rephrases the question},
under a hard semantic-equivalence constraint, move a sampling-based detector's score
in a chosen direction while the model's answer---and its correctness---stays put?

Answering this question rigorously turns out to require controls that prior
evaluations do not jointly apply, and once they are applied the picture is more subtle
than a clean ``we broke it.'' We contribute those controls as a protocol and report
what they reveal. Three are load-bearing: (i) targets are selected
\emph{score-independently}, or the clean detector is evaluated on a pool its own scores
defined---a circularity that inflates clean AUROC to $1.0$; (ii) an attack counts only
if the model's \emph{hallucination status} is unchanged under the paraphrase, checked
with the same oracle that labelled the data; and (iii) effect sizes are reported
\emph{net of a benign-paraphrase noise floor}, since the detector score is a
finite-sample estimate and an optimiser selecting the extremum over many candidates
otherwise mistakes sampling variance for success. In a preliminary evaluation the
optimised attack moves the detector---beating benign paraphrasing as measured by the
detector's own NLI clusterer, an effect we quantify at scale in
Section~\ref{sec:experiments}---but that measure is precisely the one that cannot
separate a change in the model's answer distribution from an inconsistency of the NLI
model that the detector's clustering and the attack's equivalence gate \emph{share}.
The independent oracles one would reach for to break this tie---sentence embedders---turn
out to be near-chance on exactly the word-preserving, meaning-shifted case an attack
produces. On current evidence, then, the attribution is \emph{unresolved but bounded},
and single-clusterer paraphrase-attack claims on these detectors are not adequately
supported. We formulate the attack in \emph{both} directions---\emph{hide} designed to
suppress a wrong answer's score, the under-explored \emph{false-alarm} to inflate a
correct answer's---as the case study that exercises the protocol; only the false-alarm
direction is evaluated under the null control in this work.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(1) \textbf{A measurement-validity result about semantic entropy itself}, independent of
whether any attack succeeds. The score is bounded above by $\log N$; at the standard
$N{=}10$, a tenth of \emph{correct} answers already sit exactly at that ceiling, a quarter
sit in the top decile of the scale, and the estimator realises only $22$ distinct values
across $97$ questions. Raising $N$ does not recover the range, because the baselines rise
with the cap. A detector this compressed at the top of its scale is fragile in the
false-alarm direction before an adversary appears, and effect sizes reported in its native
units are censored on roughly half of attacked targets.
(2) \textbf{A measured account of how much of a paraphrase-attack effect is an artefact of
its own search.} Re-scoring each \emph{selected} paraphrase on an independent sample, we
find only $45\%$ of the apparent effect survives ($-0.383$ nats, $95\%$ CI
$[-0.529, -0.234]$). Half of what an uncorrected report would claim is selection on
sampling noise. That attack success scales with the search budget is
known~\citep{hughes2024bon}, as is the case that success-rate comparisons need explicit
measurement validity~\citep{chouldechova2025comparison}; what we add is the measurement for
a \emph{stochastic detector score}, where the randomness being resampled lives inside the
estimator rather than in the victim's decoding.
(3) \textbf{A protocol that makes the question answerable}: score-independent target
selection (without it the clean detector looks perfect---AUROC $1.0$ by construction versus
$0.704$ [$0.653$, $0.753$] under fair selection), an answer-invariance success criterion,
and an exceedance test that prices the attacker's search budget into the null analytically,
with tie handling that remains valid when the score saturates. Each control is individually
precedented; we are not aware of them applied jointly to a sampling-based hallucination
detector.
(4) \textbf{The independent equivalence oracle the attribution requires.} Because the
detector's NLI model both clusters answers and certifies paraphrase equivalence, a
single-clusterer evaluation cannot separate a real change in the model's answers from that
model's own inconsistency. Cheap oracles do not resolve it---sentence embedders are
near-chance on exactly the adversarial case---so we build and validate an LLM-judge that
clears it ($0.93$ vs.\ $0.51$), and report the confirmatory evaluation under it at the
pre-registered scale.
(5) \textbf{The attack itself}---bidirectional, NLI-constrained, foregrounding the
under-explored induced-false-positive direction---as the case study that exercises the
protocol, and a reformulation-averaging defense with an analysis of what it does and does
not close. Our
effect-size bracket on the fair, null-controlled pool is reported in
Section~\ref{sec:experiments}; no effect size enters the body until the definitive
$n{\geq}80$ run lands under the budget-matched null control, and the small-sample
machinery pass is treated as pipeline-validation only.
