\begin{abstract}
% Option-B (protocol-led) draft, ~210 words. Numbers finalise after the n>=80 run.
Sampling-based hallucination detectors such as semantic entropy are deployed on the
claim that the uncertainty they report is invariant to how a question is phrased. We
ask whether a meaning-preserving paraphrase can break that claim, and find that
answering rigorously is harder than prior work assumed. We introduce a null-controlled
evaluation protocol---score-independent target selection, an answer-invariance success
criterion, and a benign-paraphrase noise floor---and show each control is necessary:
without them a circular selection inflates the clean detector to perfect accuracy,
sampling noise masquerades as attack success, and meaning-changing paraphrases are
miscounted as wins. Under the protocol, a preliminary evaluation finds that an optimised
paraphrase attack moves the detector as measured by the detector's own NLI clusterer, in
both the hallucination-hiding and the under-explored false-alarm direction. But that
clusterer is shared with the attack's equivalence gate, so this measure cannot
distinguish a real change in the model's answers from the clusterer's own inconsistency;
and the cheap independent oracles one would use to break the tie are near-chance on
exactly the adversarial case. We conclude that the standard single-clusterer evaluation
cannot support such attack claims without an independent oracle, and we build and validate
one---an LLM-judge that, unlike the embedders, clears the adversarial discrimination
($0.92$ vs.\ $0.51$). Under this validated judge a preliminary pass indicates the attack's
effect largely survives the independent clusterer, which we report as a directional signal
pending confirmation at the pre-registered scale rather than as a settled claim.
\end{abstract}
