\begin{abstract}
% ~200 words (critic gate, 2026-08-13; the previous draft had accreted to ~750). Everything
% cut from here is still stated where it belongs: the three-estimator scoping and the
% Eq.(4)/Eq.(5) transfer argument in Methods, the N=20 pilot in Discussion, the judge
% validation in Methods, the interval on every rate in Discussion and Conclusion.
Sampling-based hallucination detectors such as semantic entropy are deployed on the claim
that the uncertainty they report is invariant to how a question is phrased. We set out to
break it with meaning-preserving paraphrases and found the binding constraint falls on the
operator, not the attacker. The \emph{discrete} estimator we study weights meaning clusters
by sample counts, making it the entropy of a partition of $N$: at the standard $N{=}10$ it
takes only $39$ values, with an atom at the maximum. Every threshold above that maximum
flags nothing, so the lowest false-alarm rate at which a detector fires at all \emph{is} the
chance that a clean correct answer yields $N$ mutually distinct meanings---$10.5\%$
[$9.0$, $12.2$] over $1424$ clean correct answers. An operator who specifies a $5\%$
false-alarm budget cannot have one, however well the score ranks. Measuring an attack here
needs stronger controls than this literature's random-perturbation baseline: re-scoring each
selected paraphrase on an independent sample retains only $45\%$ [$25\%$, $65\%$] of the
apparent effect, so roughly half of an uncorrected result is selection on sampling noise. We
give a protocol that makes the question answerable: score-independent target selection, an
answer-invariance success criterion, a null priced against the attacker's search budget, and
an independent LLM-judge equivalence oracle. The confirmatory attack evaluation under it is
under way; we claim no attack effect here.
\end{abstract}
