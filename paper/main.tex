\begin{abstract}
% Option-B (protocol-led) draft, ~210 words. Numbers finalise after the n>=80 run.
Sampling-based hallucination detectors such as semantic entropy are deployed on the claim
that the uncertainty they report is invariant to how a question is phrased. We set out to
break that claim with meaning-preserving paraphrases and found, first, that the detector has
very little room in which to be broken. Semantic entropy over $N$ sampled answers is bounded
above by $\log N$; at the standard $N{=}10$ we find that a tenth of \emph{correct} answers
already sit exactly at that ceiling before any attack, a quarter sit in the top decile of
the scale, and the estimator takes only $22$ distinct values across $97$ questions. An
optimised paraphrase attack drives a further $39\%$ of correct answers onto the ceiling, but
so does ordinary rephrasing, and raising $N$ does not help because the baseline rises with
the cap. Second, we show that measuring such an attack honestly is harder than prior work
assumes. The attack reports a maximum over $\sim$$181$ candidates, and re-scoring each
\emph{selected} paraphrase on an independent sample shows that only $45\%$ of its apparent
effect survives ($-0.383$ nats, $95\%$ CI $[-0.529,-0.234]$): about half of what a naive
report would claim is selection on sampling noise. We therefore give a protocol that makes
the question answerable---score-independent target selection, an answer-invariance success
criterion, and an exceedance test that prices the attacker's search budget into the null
analytically, with tie handling that survives the ceiling---together with the independent
equivalence oracle the attribution requires, an LLM-judge that clears the adversarial
discrimination on which sentence embedders are near-chance ($0.93$ vs.\ $0.51$). We report
the confirmatory evaluation at the pre-registered scale under this protocol. The broader
point is that a detector need not be badly calibrated to be fragile: this one separates
correct from hallucinating answers moderately well overall (AUROC $0.70$ on our
score-independent pool), yet has so little usable range at the \emph{top} of its scale---the
region a false alarm must reach---that the direction is compromised for reasons that precede
any adversary.
\end{abstract}
