\begin{abstract}
% Option-B (protocol-led) draft, ~210 words. Numbers finalise after the n>=80 run.
Sampling-based hallucination detectors such as semantic entropy are deployed on the claim
that the uncertainty they report is invariant to how a question is phrased. We set out to
break that claim with meaning-preserving paraphrases and found, first, that the detector has
very little room in which to be broken. Semantic entropy over $N$ sampled answers is bounded
above by $\log N$; at the standard $N{=}10$, across the $97$ targets of our attack campaign
scored clean, a tenth of \emph{correct} answers already sit exactly at that ceiling before
any attack, a quarter sit in the top tenth of the score's range, and the estimator takes only $22$
distinct values in all. An optimised paraphrase attack drives a further $42\%$ onto the
ceiling, taking the total there past half; whether ordinary rephrasing does the same is
precisely what a budget-matched null control has to settle, and we do not yet claim it.
Raising $N$ does not help, because the baseline rises with the cap---on the $15$ saturated
targets we re-scored at $N{=}20$, lifting the cap by $0.693$ nats left $20\%$ still pinned
and the mean attack move essentially unchanged. Second, the controls this measurement needs
are stronger than the random-perturbation baseline that is standard in this literature. The
attack reports a maximum over $\sim$$181$ candidates, and re-scoring each
\emph{selected} paraphrase on an independent sample shows the effect shrinks
significantly---by $-0.383$ nats, $95\%$ CI $[-0.529,-0.234]$---with a point retention of
$45\%$ and a wide interval of $[25\%, 65\%]$: that inflation is present is established,
though its exact size is not, and roughly half of what a naive report would claim is
selection on sampling noise. We therefore give a protocol that makes
the question answerable---score-independent target selection, an answer-invariance success
criterion, and an exceedance test that prices the attacker's search budget into the null
analytically, with tie handling that survives the ceiling---together with the independent
equivalence oracle the attribution requires, an LLM-judge that clears the adversarial
discrimination on which sentence embedders are near-chance ($0.93$ vs.\ $0.51$ on a
domain-matched gold-alias proxy; validation on messy real sampled answers remains owed). The
confirmatory evaluation at the pre-registered scale is under way under this protocol. The
broader point is that a detector need not be badly calibrated to be fragile: on a separate
score-independent \emph{fair} pool ($200$ correct, $200$ hallucinating) this one separates
the two moderately well (AUROC $0.70$), yet has so little usable range at the \emph{top} of
its scale---the
region a false alarm must reach---that the direction is compromised for reasons that precede
any adversary.
\end{abstract}
